FBISE EXAM PREPARATION

How to Prepare for FBISE Exams: SLO-Based Study Routine

A practical study guide for Grade 9, Grade 10, 1st Year and 2nd Year students. Build your preparation around the syllabus, SLOs, assessment resources, model papers, past papers and regular revision.

Start with official resources: FBISE publishes syllabus/model-paper resources and Assessment Frameworks for SSC and HSSC.
Assessment & Model PapersSyllabus

1. Start with the FBISE syllabus

Make a subject-wise list of chapters and topics. Mark each as not started, learning, revised or strong. This gives you a realistic picture of your preparation.

2. Study according to SLOs

SLO means Students Learning Outcomes. Instead of memorizing only predicted questions, focus on what you are expected to understand, explain, apply or solve. Use the current official Assessment Framework for your subject.

3. Practise MCQs and descriptive questions

Do not prepare only objective questions. Mix MCQs, short questions, long questions, numericals, diagrams and other question types relevant to your subject.

4. Use model papers correctly

Model papers are useful for understanding assessment style and practising question formats. They are not a promise that identical questions will appear in the final examination.

5. Add old papers

Once you understand the current framework, use previous papers under timed conditions. Review every mistake and identify whether it came from knowledge, misunderstanding, carelessness or time management.

6. Simple daily routine

SessionFocus
MorningRevision + difficult concept
MiddaySecond subject + notes
AfternoonMCQs + short questions
EveningNumericals / long questions / practice
NightQuick revision + next-day plan

7. 30-day revision plan

  • Days 1–15: Complete unfinished topics and build understanding.
  • Days 16–22: Revise and target weak chapters.
  • Days 23–27: Attempt model and past papers with a timer.
  • Days 28–30: Review mistakes, formulas, definitions, MCQs and difficult areas.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Relying only on guess papers.
  • Ignoring the current syllabus or assessment framework.
  • Memorizing without understanding.
  • Leaving weak subjects until the end.
  • Never practising with a timer.
  • Using outdated exam information without checking the official source.
